Introduction

From: Brighton, UK
For Fans Of: Glassjaw, Thrice, At The Drive-In, Deftones

Since the band's inception, Windowhead's aim has been to create its own unique musical vision by incorporating different textures and approaches from a wide variety of musical styles. This goal was initially realised in 2018's Still Life, which combined the huge crescendos of Post-Rock with the hazy acoustic guitars of Lo-Fi Indie. On its follow-up album Sunflowers (2021), the band developed its sound by incorporating influences from Post-Hardcore, Shoegaze and Metal, juxtaposing a dreamlike atmosphere of swirling, ethereal guitars and layered vocals with the sinister edge provided by thundering drums and bass.

Windowhead’s next release was the culmination of 3 years spent honing its craft as a live band. As a result, the Terrestrials EP (2025) inherited an increased focus on immediacy from Post-Hardcore influences such as Thrice, Glassjaw and At The Drive-In, combining the band's signature effects-heavy guitars with unconventional time signatures, extreme dynamics and chaotic rhythms. The EP received glowing reviews from a range of publications and ranked on end-of-year lists including Noizze’s top 20 EPs and Vinyl Fantasy’s top 50 songs.

Following up on this success is Windowhead’s next release; You Will Never Be Erased. Due for release as a standalone single October 2026, it also acts as a taste of things to come in the band’s future.

You Will Never Be Erased

Windowhead’s latest track, You Will Never Be Erased, sees the group continuing to develop its focus on immediacy, which manifests here in a naturalistic “four people in a room” sound. This enables the emotion behind the song to take centre stage, with lyrics that not only mourn the loss of a loved one, but also contemplate the fear of one day forgetting them.

Musically, the song leans into Windowhead’s more Shoegaze-focused influences such as Whirr and Hum, while retaining a Post-Rock sensibility in its atmospheric climax. The band is therefore able to continue to explore shifting dynamics and the contrast between ethereal soundscapes and crushing climaxes, but with a greater focus on hooks. Windowhead hopes that taking this more naturalistic approach will allow audiences to relate to the song, creating moments of genuine catharsis for those who have gone through similar experiences.
